# Low-altitude economy scenario strategy brief

**Decision:** Which scenarios should an internet or technology platform enter first?

**Recommendation:** Start with urban instant-delivery pilots and an asset-light digital-services layer. Pursue industrial inspection, urban governance, and medical logistics through selected partnerships. Monitor passenger mobility rather than leading with aircraft or vertiport investment.

## Why now

Official civil-aviation data show that low-altitude operations are no longer only experimental. By the end of 2025, China had 3.287 million registered drones. Drone flight hours reached 45.3029 million in 2025, up 69.89% year on year, and 46 low-altitude flight service stations covered 23 provincial-level regions.

Policy also makes the constraints explicit. The 2026 low-altitude insurance opinion names logistics, inspection, governance, emergency response, medical rescue, tourism, and transport, while calling for responsibility insurance, risk monitoring, data standards, and information-platform connections. Opportunity and compliance therefore need to be evaluated together.

## Portfolio recommendation

### 1. Urban instant delivery: pilot now

The platform has direct advantages in demand aggregation, merchant relationships, payments, dispatch, routing, and customer service. Meituan's company-reported scale of more than one million drone delivery orders and 65 urban routes by July 2026 provides evidence that the operating model can move beyond a single demonstration.

The first test should be narrow: one restricted route, a small merchant set, clear weather and payload limits, and a ground-delivery fallback. The decision gate is not order volume alone. It should combine cost per completed order, on-time performance, incident rate, route utilization, and repeat use.

### 2. Digital infrastructure and services: build through partnerships

Dispatch, risk, map, weather, identity, payment, insurance, and operating-data services can support several low-altitude scenarios without owning aircraft. This direction fits a platform's existing capabilities and reduces the risk of making an early hardware bet.

The first product should solve one operator workflow, such as route authorization, risk assessment, dispatch, or insurance evidence. A local flight-service station and one licensed operator are sufficient for an initial co-development pilot.

### 3. Selective B2B and public-sector scenarios

Industrial inspection has clearer enterprise payers and more manageable operating environments than passenger mobility. Urban governance and emergency response fit maps, cloud, AI, and command systems but depend on procurement and cross-agency data governance. Medical logistics has strong value where ground transport is slow or fragile, but it requires chain-of-custody, temperature control, reliability, and fallback procedures.

These scenarios should enter the portfolio only when a partner brings domain workflow, operating permission, and a measurable service-level problem.

### 4. Passenger mobility: monitor

Passenger eVTOL has long-term potential and high visibility. It is a poor first move for a general platform because airworthiness, operator certification, vertiports, insurance, public acceptance, emergency response, and capital intensity all need to work together. The platform can learn through route research, ticketing, maps, or travel distribution without committing to aircraft ownership.

## 90-day action plan

| Period | Action | Output | Decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–30 | Select one city, route, licensed operator, and merchant or institutional partner | Baseline demand, permissions map, safety responsibilities, unit-economics assumptions | Stop if no constrained route has both demand density and an executable permission path |
| Days 31–60 | Build dispatch, customer notification, operating dashboard, and ground fallback | End-to-end pilot workflow | Stop if integration cannot produce traceable orders and incident reporting |
| Days 61–90 | Run limited operations and compare with the ground baseline | Cost, time, reliability, safety, utilization, and repeat-use results | Scale only if reliability and safety thresholds hold and the economics improve with plausible density |

## Key uncertainty

The ranking is most sensitive to local regulatory feasibility and the platform's real ability to use existing merchant and dispatch assets. A city-level pilot design should therefore replace national averages with route-specific permission, weather, infrastructure, and cost evidence before any capital commitment.
