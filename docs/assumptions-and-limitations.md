# Assumptions and limitations

## Assumptions

- The decision owner is an internet or technology platform, not an aircraft manufacturer or licensed airline.
- The first decision is where to test, not where to make a nationwide capital commitment.
- Partnerships with licensed operators, infrastructure providers, insurers, and local governments are available.
- Scores reflect China as the primary market as of 13 September 2026.
- Platform fit includes user demand, merchants, payments, maps, cloud, AI, dispatch, and operations.

## Limitations

- The project does not estimate total addressable market or discounted cash flow.
- Company figures are self-reported and may use different operating definitions.
- Regulations, local pilot rules, airspace permissions, and insurance requirements vary by location and can change.
- The scoring model compares strategic attractiveness; it does not prove causality or guarantee commercial success.
- Equal numeric intervals between scores are a simplification. The difference between 4 and 5 is a judgment, not a measured unit.
- Passenger mobility and critical logistics require safety, airworthiness, operator, and emergency-response diligence beyond this portfolio model.

## What would be required before a real investment decision

1. City- and route-level regulatory review
2. Unit-economics model with ground-delivery comparison
3. Safety case, insurance scope, and incident-response design
4. Partner diligence and technical integration assessment
5. Customer interviews and willingness-to-pay evidence
6. Pilot success thresholds and explicit stop conditions
